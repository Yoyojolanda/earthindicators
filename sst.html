Interpretation

    Daily sea-surface temperature averaged over the oceans between 60°S and 60°N (area-weighted, every fourth 0.25° grid point, i.e. a 1° sample). Anomaly = daily SST minus the 1991–2020 daily mean. Recent values are preliminary and may be revised by NOAA. Data: NOAA OISST v2.1, provided by the NOAA PSL, Boulder, Colorado, USA, from their website at psl.noaa.gov.

    Marine heatwaves

    A marine heatwave is a spell of at least five days in a row when the sea at a place is warmer than it was on 9 days in 10 at that time of year in 1991–2020. Like heatwaves on land, they kill: fish, kelp forests, seagrass, seabirds and corals die off, and fisheries collapse. Below, how much of the ocean is in a heatwave on each day.

     

      Definition: the standard one by Hobday et al. (2016). For every point of the ocean and every day of the year, the "threshold" is the temperature exceeded on 10% of the days within ±5 days of that date in 1991–2020; a heatwave is 5 or more days in a row above it, and spells less than 3 days apart count as one. Categories (Hobday et al. 2018) say how far above normal the water is, in multiples of the gap between the threshold and the 1991–2020 average for the date: 1–2 times moderate, 2–3 strong, 3–4 severe, 4 or more extreme. The threshold stays fixed to 1991–2020, so a warming ocean spends more and more days above it; that is what the chart shows. Calculated here from NOAA OISST v2.1 daily sea-surface temperature (satellites, ships and buoys), on a 2° sample of its 0.25° grid between 60°S and 60°N, weighted by area; days when a point is in its sea-ice season are not counted. Published studies use the full grid, so their percentages differ a little, but the pattern is the same. The newest days are preliminary for about two weeks. The last 4 days (dashed) are not settled yet and always read too low: a heatwave only counts once it has lasted 5 days, and a dip of 1–2 days below the threshold can still turn out to be part of an ongoing heatwave. They are revised upward as new days come in, so the cards and text use the last settled day.
