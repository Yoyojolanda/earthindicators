The map: every ocean

The charts above average many grid points into one number. The map shows each of them: the latest day (preliminary) or the last complete month, as the temperature itself or compared with the 1991–2020 normal for the same date. Drag across the map (pinch on a phone) to zoom in; double-click or "Reset zoom" to see the whole globe again.

NOAA OISST v2.1 (NOAA PSL), every 1° (every 4th point of the 0.25° grid). "Compared with normal" is the difference from NOAA's 1991–2020 average for the same day of the year (latest day) or calendar month. The latest day is preliminary: NOAA revises it over about two weeks. The picture blends neighboring grid points; the value shown when you point at the map is the grid point's own. Gray is land (Natural Earth outline), near-white is sea ice (at least 15% ice cover, from NOAA's ice data in the same product). Robinson projection centered on 160°W, like Climate Reanalyzer's map; the dashed box is Niño 3.4 (El Niño page, which also compares the Pacific with past El Niños). How the map is made.

Interpretation

    Daily sea-surface temperature averaged over the oceans between 60°S and 60°N (area-weighted, every fourth 0.25° grid point, i.e. a 1° sample). Anomaly = daily SST minus the 1991–2020 daily mean. Recent values are preliminary and may be revised by NOAA. Data: NOAA OISST v2.1, provided by the NOAA PSL, Boulder, Colorado, USA, from their website at psl.noaa.gov.
