Planetary boundaries

Climate change is one of nine ways people are pushing the Earth system. For each, scientists have set a boundary: a level that kept the planet in the stable state of the last 10,000 years, with a margin for what is not yet known. Past the boundary lies a zone of increasing risk, and beyond that a high-risk zone. This page shows the latest assessment, the Planetary Health Check 2026, and marks which numbers Earth Indicators measures itself.

The report counts 13 control variables; phosphorus has two measures, a global one and a regional one, shown here as separate rows. How the chart is scaled: each bar runs from the pre-industrial or Holocene-like reference (0) to the boundary (1) in a straight line, then from the boundary to the start of the high-risk zone (2), then on at the same size per step beyond it; values far past that are cut off at the right edge and labelled with their real number. Measures where lower is worse (forest, aragonite, ozone) are turned around so that longer always means worse. A range (extinctions, plant growth taken by people) is drawn solid to its low end and hatched to its high end. Novel entities have no numeric measure: the boundary is zero untested release, and it is clearly crossed, so that row has no length of its own. The CO₂ bar uses the latest 12 months of NOAA's Mauna Loa record (CO₂ page) instead of the report's 426 ppm. The site's own pages measure related things in a different way, so they are linked but not swapped in: greenhouse-gas forcing on the other gases page (the boundary counts all human forcing, including aerosols), ocean pH near Hawaiʻi and tree cover loss on the slow signals page, and sulfur dioxide on the energy imbalance page.
