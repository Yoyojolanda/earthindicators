Interpretation

    Where does the heat go? Satellites compared with the ocean

    The satellites measure how much energy Earth gains in total. Most of it should turn up in the ocean, which floats measure from the surface down to 2000 m. Both over the same 10 years, :

    Energy imbalance = absorbed solar radiation (incoming minus reflected sunlight) minus outgoing longwave (heat) radiation, at the top of the atmosphere, averaged over the whole Earth (510 million km²). Positive means Earth is gaining heat. Running means end at the month shown. CERES EBAF anchors the absolute level of the imbalance to ocean heat content measurements; changes over time come from the satellite observations themselves. Monthly values are published with a lag of a few months and may be revised. Data: NASA CERES EBAF-TOA Edition 4.2.1 (ceres.larc.nasa.gov), NASA Langley Research Center, distributed by the NASA Atmospheric Science Data Center.

    Further reading: Hansen et al. (2023), “Global warming in the pipeline”, Oxford Open Climate Change, with Leon Simons among the co-authors. They argue that the cut in aerosol pollution, especially the sulfur rules for shipping, is a large part of why the imbalance has grown so fast, and that warming is speeding up as a result. Other scientists think they overestimate the aerosol effect; the debate is about how large it is, not whether it exists.
