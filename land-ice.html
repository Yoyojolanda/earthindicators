Interpretation

    The GRACE (2002–2017) and GRACE Follow-On (since 2018) satellites measure tiny changes in Earth's gravity; less ice means slightly less pull. The values are mass changes in gigatonnes (1 Gt = 1 billion tonnes, about 1 km³ of water), shown here relative to the first 12 months of the record. Between the two missions there is a gap of about a year (mid-2017 to mid-2018). Sea-level equivalent: 362 Gt of ice spread over the world's oceans raises sea level by about 1 mm. Gaps: the original GRACE satellites lost battery capacity from 2011, so they were switched off for a month or two every few months to save power; the mission ended in 2017 and its successor, GRACE-FO, started delivering data in mid-2018, with a few more months missing in its first months. Lines are broken across gaps of more than about three months. Yearly change = the average of one year minus the average of the year before; for this, gaps of up to three months are filled by straight-line interpolation, and only years with at least ten months count. Mountain glaciers are not included yet. Data: NASA JPL GRACE/GRACE-FO mascon time series, release 06.3 version 04: Greenland (doi:10.5067/TEMSC-GT634) and Antarctica (doi:10.5067/TEMSC-AT634).
